The finding

Buy the three strongest sectors of the last six months, short the three weakest, rebalance monthly. Over 27 years that spread returned +0.130% per month before costs — and it is not distinguishable from zero.

02

What was tested

Every parameter was fixed before the data was run. No grid search over lookbacks, holding periods, or leg sizes — searching would have made any resulting t-statistic uninterpretable.

Universe
The nine original Select Sector SPDRs — XLK, XLF, XLE, XLV, XLY, XLP, XLI, XLU, XLB. All launched 16 Dec 1998, so the panel is balanced. XLRE and XLC are excluded on purpose: including them would truncate the sample by two decades.
Signal
Cumulative return over the 6-month window ending two months before the holding month. The 6-month formation is one of Jegadeesh & Titman's original J values; the skipped month follows the standard convention for keeping short-term reversal out of a medium-term signal.
Portfolio
Long the top 3 by signal, short the bottom 3, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.
Benchmarks
Buy-and-hold SPY, and an equal-weight basket of all nine sectors — so the test isolates selection from simply being invested.

04

How much does this null actually tell us?

Very little — and this is the most important thing on the page.

A null result is only informative to the extent the test could have rejected. With nine assets and a top-three/bottom-three sort, the spread's monthly standard deviation is 4.07%. That puts the minimum detectable effect at +0.63% per month — +7.8% a year, an effect larger than classic stock-level momentum ever produced on extreme deciles of thousands of names.

A nine-asset sort cannot generate spreads that extreme; its winners and losers sit close to the middle of the distribution. So the plausible effect size lands squarely in the region where this test is nearly blind.

The null was substantially determined by the design, not discovered from the market. This is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — and the confidence interval says so plainly: it contains +0.5%/month, an effect anyone would happily trade.

05

The raw mean was measuring the wrong thing

Regressing the spread on the market reveals a persistent short-market tilt. Momentum is long defensives and short cyclicals more often than the reverse, and over a sample where the market compounded at 8.5% a year, that tilt was expensive.

Decomposition — exact to 1×10−18 alpha +0.371% (t = +2.33)
  + beta −0.309 (t = −4.33) × market +0.780%
  = +0.130% ← the raw monthly mean

Strip the beta out and the residual alpha is +0.371% a month, t = 2.33 — nominally significant. It is tempting to lead with that number. Four reasons not to:

  1. It was not pre-specified. This regression was run only after seeing the null — the exact researcher degree of freedom the study was built to avoid. There is no honest multiple-testing correction, because there is no way to enumerate the alternatives that would have been tried had this one also come back flat.
  2. Beta is unstable, and the model assumes it is constant. By regime: −0.32, −0.38, +0.01, −0.35. A constant-beta regression is the wrong specification for a series whose beta visibly moves.
  3. No subperiod is individually significant, and the signs flip. See below.
  4. It decays under costs. At 5 bps the alpha is +0.313% (t = 1.96), right on the boundary; at 10 bps, +0.256% (t = 1.60) — before the short-leg borrow carry.

Caught a real bug

pandas .autocorr() is not the ACF

It computes a pairwise correlation over the overlapping subset. The Ljung–Box statistic was 15.67 (p = 0.0156); corrected, it is 15.03 (p = 0.0200).

Recomputing the signal with every observation after month t−2 deleted must return the identical value. It does.

Behavioral

The worst month is the documented 2009 momentum crash; the best is the March 2020 energy collapse. A sign-flipped or misaligned implementation would not produce both.

07

What this does not settle

  • The post-hoc alpha cannot be assessed on this sample. That is what post-hoc means; no further work here can fix it.
  • Short borrow rates are assumed, not measured. Reported as a 0–100 bp/yr sensitivity, since historical rates come from securities-lending vendors and are not public.
  • Turnover ignores intra-month weight drift, mildly understating costs.
  • The universe stops spanning the market after 2018. Excluding XLC is right for panel balance, but it omits a large and fast-growing slice of the index.

The one thing that would change the answer

Both open threads — the untrustworthy alpha and the power problem — have the same fix: a longer, finer cross-section. Fama–French industry portfolios run back to 1926 in 30- and 48-industry versions. That is roughly four times the sample and a cross-section fine enough for genuine decile sorts, which raises power substantially. The pre-1999 period is also data this sample cannot have contaminated — making it a real out-of-sample test of the alpha rather than another look at the same observations.

Until then, the reportable result is the one in section 01: no effect detected.
